Answers & Rationales
1.	C. The endoplasmic reticulum is responsible for protein synthesis.
A. Centrioles are involved in cell division.
B. Cilium is involved in motion, or movement of substances, past the ciliated cell.
D. Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes. 

2. 	A. Bleeding is not a cardinal sign of infection.
	B. Heat is a cardinal sign of infection.
	C. Pain is a cardinal sign of infection.
	D. Redness is a cardinal sign of infection.

3.	D. A chronic infection has a slow onset and long duration.
	A. A primary infection is the origin of the infection.
	B. A focal infection is localized in one area and spreads elsewhere in the body from that point.
	C. An acute infection runs a rapid course and terminates abruptly.

4. 	A. HIV is not transmitted through sharing utensils with an HIV positive person.
	B. HIV can be transmitted through intravenous drug use.
	C. HIV can be transmitted through sexual contact with an HIV positive person.
	D. HIV can be transmitted from mother to child (perinatally).

5.	B. Warts are not an oral manifestation of Human Immunodeficiency Virus.
	A. Recurrent apthous ulcers are an oral manifestation of Human Immunodeficiency Virus.
	C. Kaposi’s sarcoma is an oral manifestation of Human Immunodeficiency Virus.
	D. Hairy leukoplakia is an oral manifestation of Human Immunodeficiency Virus.

6.	C. Papillomaviruses cause warts.
	A. The Epstein-Barr virus causes infectious mononucleosis
	B. N. gonnorrhoeae is the causative agent in gonnorhea.
	D. Rubella virus is the causative agent in German measles.

7. 	A. Fungi are eukaryotic.
	B. Viruses are prokaryotic.	
	C. Eubacteria are prokaryotic.
	D. Archeobacteria are prokaryotic.

8.	D. Darkfield microscopy allows specimens to be seen as bright objects against a dark background.
	A. Phase-contrast microscopy is useful in examining live specimens.
	B. Bright-field microscopy is used to observe the morphology of microorganisms that are stained.
	C. Fluorescence microscopy is used to visualize objects that fluoresce when exposed to light of different wavelengths.

9. 	B. A child receives immunity from the mother through passive immunity.
	A. Cell-mediated immunity involves the stimulation of T lymphocytes to activate a variety of effector T cell in response to an antigen.
	C. Active acquired immunity occurs when a person is exposed to an antigen and the body produces antibodies.
	D. Humoral immunity involves B lymphocyte production and the release of specific antibodies into the bloodstream. The antibodies protect the host.

10. 	C. Polymorphonuclear leukocytes are the first immune cells to respond to an infection.
	A. B-lympocytes make antibodies.
	B. T-lympocytes intensify the immune reaction by releasing cytokines.

11.	B. Toxemia is the presence of toxins in the bloodstream.
	A. Bacteremia is the presence of bacteria in the bloodstream.
	C. Nosocomial infections are hospital-acquired infections.

12.	D. Toluidine blue staining is used to determine the presence of prions, proteoglycans, and    glycosaminoglycans present in tissue.
	A. Negative staining is used to determine if an organism is encapsulated.
	B. Schaeffer-Fulton staining is used to determine if an organism is a spore former.
	C. Flagellar staining is used to determine is an organism has flagella.

	A. Streptobacilli appear rod like and occur in chains.
