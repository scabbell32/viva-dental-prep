## 1. Regulatory Agencies, Guidelines & Professional Bodies
* **OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration)**:
  * Division of the US Department of Labor. **Federal and state regulatory enforcement body**.
  * Authority to formulate and **enforce workplace safety regulations** (Bloodborne Pathogens Standard, hazard communication, PPE, sharps safety).
  * Requires employers to provide post-exposure evaluation and follow-up at **no cost to employees**. Does NOT mandate paid time off or pay for medical treatment (handled via Worker's Compensation).
* **CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)**:
  * Advisory public health agency under DHHS. Formulates **evidence-based recommendations** and guidelines for infection prevention in healthcare settings (not an enforcement agency).
* **FDA (Food and Drug Administration)**:
  * Regulates the safety and efficacy of **medical devices**, sterilizers, ultrasonic cleaners, gloves, surgical masks, and chemical sterilants/disinfectants.
* **EPA (Environmental Protection Agency)**:
  * Regulates hospital disinfectants and environmental waste management.
* **ADS (Association for Dental Safety, formerly OSAP)**:
  * Promotes science-based dental infection control policies and safety education.

> [!NOTE]
> **HIGH-YIELD FOR QUESTIONS 1, 2, 33, 39, 41, 42:**
> * **OSHA** is a **regulatory enforcement body** that enforces workplace safety laws.
> * The **CDC** formulates **evidence-based recommendations** for disease prevention.
> * The **FDA** regulates the safety and effectiveness of **sterilizers and ultrasonic units**.
> * Employers must provide post-exposure medical follow-up at **no cost to the employee**.

## 2. Chain of Infection & Transmission Pathways
* **Elements of Chain of Infection**: Pathogen (source) → Reservoir → Portal of exit → Mode of transmission → Portal of entry → Susceptible host.
* **Pathways of Disease Transmission**:
  * **Direct Contact**: Person-to-person physical exposure of nonintact skin or mucous membranes to infected blood or bodily fluids (e.g., bare hands touching blood).
  * **Indirect Contact**: Contact with contaminated inanimate objects or instruments (e.g., **sharps injury with contaminated explorer/scaler**, touching contaminated surface).
  * **Droplet Transmission**: Exposure of mucous membranes (eyes, nose, mouth) to larger spatter or droplets generated during coughing, sneezing, or ultrasonic scaling at close range (within 14–18 inches).
  * **Airborne Infection**: Inhalation of microscopic aerosolized droplets or water vapor suspended in air (e.g., **aspirating *Legionella* water vapor from DUWL** or *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*).

> [!NOTE]
> **HIGH-YIELD FOR QUESTIONS 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 15, 39, 40 & CASE STUDY A:**
> * **A sneeze or cough** represents a **portal of exit** (and portal of entry into a new host).
> * **Indirect contact** involves exposure via contaminated instruments or sharps injuries.
> * **Airborne infection** occurs via inhalation of aerosolized particles (e.g., *Legionella* in water vapor).
> * **Standard Precautions** treat blood, **all body fluids (except sweat)**, nonintact skin, and mucous membranes as potentially infectious.
> * Patients with **active tuberculosis** must be treated in an **airborne isolation room** (not a standard dental operatory).

## 3. Spaulding Classification & Instrument Processing Protocols
* **Spaulding Classification System**:
  * **Critical Items**: Penetrate soft tissue, bone, or bloodstream (e.g., **dental hygiene curettes, scalers, extraction forceps, surgical burs**). Must be **heat sterilized**.
  * **Semicritical Items**: Touch mucous membranes or nonintact skin without penetrating soft tissue or bone (e.g., **mouth mirrors, impression trays, radiograph sensors/film holders**). Must be heat sterilized or processed with high-level disinfectant.
  * **Noncritical Items**: Touch intact skin only (e.g., **blood pressure cuffs, X-ray tube heads, pulse oximeters**). Processed with low- to intermediate-level disinfectant.
* **Cleaning & Sterilization Protocols**:
  * **Pre-cleaning Bioburden**: Automated cleaning (ultrasonic bath, instrument washer) is preferred over hand scrubbing. If hand scrubbing is required, use a long-handled brush (short-handled brush is an EXCEPTION/improper technique).
  * **Wicking**: Wet sterile packages can draw environmental microbes into the package via capillary action (**wicking**). Instruments MUST be completely dry before packaging/sterilization.

> [!NOTE]
> **HIGH-YIELD FOR QUESTIONS 4, 5, 6, 16, 27, 44:**
> * **Critical items** (curettes, scalers, forceps) penetrate soft tissue and **must be heat sterilized**.
> * **Semicritical items** (mouth mirrors, X-ray sensors) touch mucous membranes.
> * **Noncritical items** (blood pressure cuff) touch intact skin only.
> * Hand scrubbing with a short-handled brush is an **unacceptable cleaning method**.
> * **Wicking** occurs when wet instrument packages absorb microbial contamination.

## 4. Environmental Surface Disinfection & Controls
* **Environmental Surface Categories**:
  * **Clinical Contact Surfaces**: High-touch surfaces in patient care area (light handles, switches, air/water syringe buttons, handpiece tubing, computer keyboards). Must be protected with surface barriers or cleaned/disinfected between patients with an **intermediate-level disinfectant**.
  * **Housekeeping Surfaces**: Floors, walls, sinks. Cleaned regularly with soap/water or low-level disinfectant.
* **Disinfectant Levels & Kill Claims**:
  * **High-Level Disinfectant**: Kills all micro-organisms including bacterial endospores (used as chemical sterilant for heat-sensitive semicritical items, e.g., glutaraldehyde).
  * **Intermediate-Level Disinfectant**: Hospital disinfectant with a **tuberculocidal claim** (kills *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, HBV, HIV, *S. aureus*). Does NOT kill *C. difficile* spores.
  * **Low-Level Disinfectant**: Hospital disinfectant without tuberculocidal claim.
* **Engineering vs. Work Practice Controls**:
  * **Engineering Controls**: Technology or devices that isolate/remove bloodborne hazards (e.g., **sharps containers, self-sheathing needles, needle recapping devices**).
  * **Work Practice Controls**: Behaviors/actions that reduce risk (e.g., **one-handed scoop recapping technique**, maintaining working distance, fulcruming safely).

> [!NOTE]
> **HIGH-YIELD FOR QUESTIONS 9, 10, 19, 20, 21, 23:**
> * **Engineering controls** are devices (e.g., sharps containers, self-sheathing needles) that isolate hazards.
> * **One-handed scoop technique** is a **work practice control**.
> * **Intermediate-level disinfectants** must carry a **tuberculocidal kill claim**; they do NOT kill *C. difficile*.
> * Housekeeping surfaces (floors) are NOT clinical contact surfaces.

## 5. Dental Unit Waterlines (DUWL) & Legionella (Case Study A)
* **DUWL Biofilm Formation**: Stagnant water in narrow DUWL promotes microbial biofilm accumulation containing opportunistic pathogens like *Legionella pneumophila* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.
* **Legionnaires' Disease (Case Study A)**:
  * **Case Scenario**: An elderly patient contracts Legionnaires' disease after dental treatment in an operatory without treated waterlines.
  * **Pathway of Transmission**: **Community-to-patient** via airborne inhalation of contaminated water aerosols/vapor.
  * **Portal of Entry**: Inhalation of water vapor into lungs (**airborne infection**).
  * **Prevention**: Dental unit waterlines must be treated and tested to ensure output water contains **<= 500 CFU/mL** of heterotrophic mesophilic water bacteria.

> [!NOTE]
> **HIGH-YIELD FOR CASE A (QUESTIONS 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6):**
> * Legionnaires' disease in Case A resulted from **community-to-patient** transmission via **untreated dental unit waterlines**.
> * The portal of entry was **inhalation of aerosolized water vapor** into the lungs.
> * *Legionella* is a **bacterium** naturally present in municipal water.
> * Waterline treatment ensures water quality stays **<= 500 CFU/mL**.

## 6. Occupational Exposure Management, Immunizations & Work Restrictions (Case Study B)
* **Post-Exposure Sharps Protocol (Case Study B)**:
  1. **Immediate First Aid**: Immediately **wash the punctured area thoroughly with soap and water**. (Do NOT scrub with a brush, squeeze the wound, or apply bleach).
  2. **Reporting & Follow-Up**: Report to Infection Control Coordinator. Employer must immediately arrange and pay for confidential medical evaluation, source patient testing, and Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP).
  3. **Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)**: PEP for HIV exposure should be initiated as soon as possible, ideally **within 2 hours** of exposure.
  4. **Bloodborne Transmission Risks**: Risk of HIV transmission after a needle stick is **< 0.3%** (very low). HCV risk is ~1.8%. HBV risk is 6–30% in unvaccinated individuals.
* **Hepatitis B Vaccination & Testing**:
  * 3-dose vaccine series. Post-vaccination serologic antibody testing (anti-HBs titer) should be performed **1–2 months after the final injection**.
* **Healthcare Worker Immunizations & Restrictions**:
  * **Recommended Vaccines**: Hepatitis B, Influenza, MMR, Varicella, Tdap, COVID-19. (Hepatitis A is NOT bloodborne and NOT routinely required).
  * **Work Restrictions**: Required for **active conjunctivitis**, **measles**, **mumps**, **rubella**, **varicella/shingles**, **active weeping dermatitis**, and **pediculosis (lice)**. General headaches do NOT require work restrictions.
  * **Medical Records**: Employee vaccination/exposure records must be kept confidentially for **duration of employment + 30 years**. (General OSHA injury logs are not required for low-hazard OHC settings unless requested).

> [!NOTE]
> **HIGH-YIELD FOR QUESTIONS 8, 13, 14, 26, 28, 30, 31, 32, 35, 36, 45, 46, 47, 48, 49, 50, 51 & CASE STUDY B:**
> * After a sharps injury, immediately **wash the wound with soap and water**.
> * **PEP for HIV** should be initiated **within 2 hours** of exposure.
> * **Hepatitis B antibody testing** should occur **1–2 months after the 3rd dose**.
> * Work restrictions are required for **conjunctivitis** and **pediculosis**, but NOT for a **headache**.
> * Employee exposure records must be maintained for **employment length + 30 years**.
> * Offices should maintain written **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)** for infection control.
